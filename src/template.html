Why the edge is the point

From the moment a lab grants pre-release access, each organization it trusts becomes part of the lab’s attack surface, and nobody publishes a measure of it. In July 2026 a three-person research team reached OpenAI’s GitHub organization and employee accounts through an image-upload flaw on its community forum, a peripheral site nobody thinks of as the vault (TechCrunch, 18 September 2026). Attackers go through whatever is attached to the building.

Each organization is read against a published code, the Frontier Perimeter Security Standard, on two independent axes: the Floor (the minimum) versus Standard Practice (hardening above it), and Level 1 (publicly observable) versus Level 2 (what only the organization can show). Nothing was supplied by any organization and nothing was tested against its systems. Every control is observed clear, an item to resolve, advisable, or not observable — never a score. Any organization can work the Level 2 self-assessment or ask for a re-measure.

No list was bought or hand-picked. Each ring comes from one public document: the Frontier AI Safety Commitments signed at the AI Seoul Summit; the external-testing sections of Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind system cards from 2024 to 2026; the organizations’ own published warnings and lab ratings; the membership of the international network of AI safety institutes; and the 666 signatories of the Collective Cyber Defense letter, from which only the AI developers and the security-ratings firms are drawn. Every inclusion sentence was read back from its source. Where the evidence was thin (a heading without a sentence, a co-authored paper, a one-person project that has stopped) the organization was held back, not included on a generous reading; the Method lists every one and why.

What this is: a Level 1 curb inspection of the organizations in the AI-risk debate, against a published code, with every reading dated, sourced and bounded on the Method page. What it is not: a security rating, a ranking, a penetration test or a finding about any organization’s internal controls. The controls that protect model weights from a capable attacker sit behind Level 2 and beyond it; a clean curb says nothing about them, and a mark below the Floor says only what it says.
